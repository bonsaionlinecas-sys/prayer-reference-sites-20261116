Travel

An excerpt from the travel dua

An excerpt from Ibn Umar's travel narration. The full report includes takbir and a longer supplication. Open the source for the complete wording and context.

اللَّهُمَّ إِنَّا نَسْأَلُكَ فِي سَفَرِنَا هَذَا الْبِرَّ وَالتَّقْوَى وَمِنَ الْعَمَلِ مَا تَرْضَى، اللَّهُمَّ هَوِّنْ عَلَيْنَا سَفَرَنَا هَذَا وَاطْوِ عَنَّا بُعْدَهُ

Meaning (editorial paraphrase): O Allah, grant us upright conduct, mindfulness of You and deeds pleasing to You on this journey. Ease its difficulty and shorten its distance for us.

Sahih Muslim 1342 ↗

Context: The request concerns the traveller's conduct as well as the journey's hardship. This explanation covers the displayed passage; the narration contains more words before and after it.

Travel

Remembrance on returning from a journey

These words are the return addition excerpted from the travel narration. Keep them with that context rather than treating them as a replacement for the rest of the reported dua.

آيِبُونَ تَائِبُونَ عَابِدُونَ لِرَبِّنَا حَامِدُونَ

Meaning (editorial paraphrase): We return in repentance, worshipping our Lord and giving Him praise.

Sahih Muslim 1342 ↗

Context: Returning is linked with repentance, worship and gratitude. The same narration gives these additional words for the return journey.

Home

Remembrance when leaving home

The remembrance within Anas's report about leaving the house. The linked source attributes its sahih grading to al-Albani; this guide does not make an independent chain assessment.

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ تَوَكَّلْتُ عَلَى اللَّهِ لاَ حَوْلَ وَلاَ قُوَّةَ إِلاَّ بِاللَّهِ

Meaning (editorial paraphrase): I leave invoking Allah's name and placing my trust in Him; every ability and strength depends on Him.

Sunan Abi Dawud 5095; sahih grading by al-Albani as listed in the source ↗

Context: The words combine naming Allah, trust in Him and recognition of dependence on His help. Practical preparation for leaving home can accompany that trust.

Match the words to the stage

Leaving the house, setting out on a journey and returning from one have distinct references. Anas's report in Sunan Abi Dawud concerns leaving home. Ibn Umar's narration in Sahih Muslim contains the travel supplication and words added on returning. A shared travel collection makes them easy to find while preserving those differences.

Start with the entry's occasion, then check whether it contains the complete passage you need. The travel entry explicitly shows an excerpt. Follow its reference for the takbir and remaining supplication, and keep the return addition identifiable when saving your own copy. A broad heading such as journey should not erase that context.

Look beyond the distance

The displayed travel passage asks for upright conduct, mindfulness of Allah and deeds that please Him, alongside an easier journey. Reading its meaning brings the purpose and conduct of the traveller into view. Take a moment to understand these requests before concentrating only on the words about the road's difficulty.

The English meaning is an editorial aid. It sits alongside the narrated Arabic so that you can study unfamiliar words without silently rewriting the quotation. When reviewing with someone who reads Arabic well, keep the source visible and ask about the phrase that is unclear rather than guessing a replacement from memory.

Prepare your reference before departure

Review the relevant entry while you are making your travel arrangements. Keep its title, hadith number and excerpt notice together. If your route crosses different cities, check the prayer schedule for the place and date where you expect to be, and consider suitable stopping places when planning the journey.

For further reading on the travel dua, use the sala-sa reference guide while preparing a shared journey note. Keep the chosen text beside its occasion and source, and ask a companion to check that any excerpt notice survives copying. Give packing reminders a separate line. If travellers have different saved copies, compare them with the cited report before deciding that a shorter passage is a wording error.

Questions about shortening or combining prayers need the details of your trip and qualified guidance. A reading collection cannot decide those matters from a travel keyword. Read the screen before moving or during a stop, and let a companion help when appropriate. Keep your attention on the road while driving.

Keep the return in its own context

The return addition links coming back with repentance, worship and praise. Learn its sense with its wording, then revisit how it relates to the longer narration. This gives you a useful way to review after the journey: identify each occasion, explain its meaning and locate its reference without relying on a title alone.

If another book presents different wording, compare the cited report and the extent of its excerpt. You may be looking at another narration or an explanation placed beside the text. Keeping a precise reference with your saved passage makes that comparison possible and helps you avoid completing an excerpt as though memory were a source.

A useful answer

Questions that help you use the guide

Is the leaving-home remembrance the same as the travel dua?

Their cited contexts differ. Sunan Abi Dawud 5095 concerns leaving the house; Sahih Muslim 1342 contains the travel narration and return addition. Read the occasion with each entry.

Does the travel entry contain the entire dua?

It contains a clearly labelled excerpt. Open Sahih Muslim 1342 for the longer supplication, the surrounding narration and the words added on returning.

Can this guide decide when I may shorten prayers?

For a personal ruling, describe your journey, intended stay and relevant circumstances to a qualified scholar. This guide explains the cited texts and helps you organise your reading.

How should I review an entry during a drive?

Read before departure or when safely stopped. A companion can help with the reference. Keep your attention on driving while the vehicle is moving.
