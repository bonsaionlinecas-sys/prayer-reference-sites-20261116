Prayer

Following the prophetic example when learning prayer

An excerpt from Malik ibn al-Huwayrith's narration. It is an instruction about learning prayer, rather than a dua to say during it.

وَصَلُّوا كَمَا رَأَيْتُمُونِي أُصَلِّي

Meaning (editorial paraphrase): Take the Prophet's own prayer as the model for learning to pray.

Sahih al-Bukhari 631 ↗

Context: The instruction connects learning prayer with the prophetic example. Reading the reports and receiving practical instruction serve that learning.

After prayer

Remembrance after completing prayer

The dua in Thawban's narration. The report mentions seeking forgiveness three times before these words; read that sequence with the displayed passage.

اللَّهُمَّ أَنْتَ السَّلاَمُ وَمِنْكَ السَّلاَمُ تَبَارَكْتَ ذَا الْجَلاَلِ وَالإِكْرَامِ

Meaning (editorial paraphrase): O Allah, You are al-Salam, and peace comes from You. Exalted in blessing are You, possessed of majesty and honour.

Sahih Muslim 591 ↗

Context: Praise invokes Allah by the name al-Salam and recognises Him as the source of peace. The narration places these words after the prayer has ended.

Build a map for learning

Malik ibn al-Huwayrith's narration connects following the Prophet's prayer with teaching one's family. A useful learning map can begin with preparation, move through the sequence of actions and their words, and include practical review with a competent teacher. This map organises study; it does not claim to list every condition or pillar of prayer.

Additional materials in the sala-sa prayer guide can help you extend your reading on how to pray salah. Distinguish a question about wording from one about movement, and note what you need a teacher to demonstrate. Keep the answer with its reference rather than treating a completed checklist as an assessment of prayer. This prepares the next lesson while leaving personal rulings to qualified guidance.

Write a precise question where your understanding breaks down. You might need help distinguishing bowing from prostration, or understanding what calmness means in a particular posture. Describe the action and your difficulty clearly. A teacher can then address the point, while a personal ruling requires the relevant details of what actually happened.

Give wudu its own lesson

Humran's report about Uthman's wudu in Sahih Muslim 226a describes a practical example of ablution. While studying it, identify the body parts and the sequence of actions described. An appropriate legal explanation helps distinguish required elements from other details of the reported practice, rather than assuming that every detail has the same classification.

Choose a learning moment when you can read and ask questions without rushing to begin prayer. If an illness, difficulty using water or limited movement affects your situation, explain that when seeking qualified advice. A short reference can help you name the subject, but applying a ruling to an individual requires more information.

Notice calmness within the sequence

Sahih al-Bukhari 757 records the teaching of a man who needed instruction in prayer. The narration describes bowing, rising, prostrating and sitting with calmness in their places. Read those transitions carefully so that learning the prayer includes more than memorising a list of movement names or rushing through their order.

Outside prayer, explain the difference between bowing, standing upright and sitting in your own words, then review your understanding with a teacher. Demonstration can clarify what a paragraph leaves difficult to picture. A drawing or a video's speed should not become an invented number of seconds that you attribute to the narration.

Distinguish the words, actions and ruling

A prayer lesson may contain recitation, remembrance, a description of movement and a legal explanation. Identify which kind of material you are reading at each point. The featured passage here instructs learners to follow the prophetic example; its role is different from the remembrance shown in the entry about finishing prayer.

Use reading to prepare questions and direct teaching to review performance. Study an amount you can understand before adding another topic. If explanations differ, ask about their evidence and the teaching approach you are following. Comparing properly described questions is more useful than combining isolated answers whose circumstances and legal contexts have disappeared.

A useful answer

Questions that help you use the guide

Is this a complete manual of prayer law?

It is an organised introduction to learning and review, with specific hadith references. Study prayer's conditions, pillars and details through reliable teaching, and ask a qualified scholar about personal circumstances.

Which reference describes calmness in prayer?

Sahih al-Bukhari 757 records instruction involving bowing, rising, prostrating and sitting with calmness. Read the full narration through the source link.

Is the featured instruction a dua to recite in prayer?

It is a teaching instruction from Malik ibn al-Huwayrith's narration in Sahih al-Bukhari 631. Its purpose here is to explain the basis of learning from the prophetic example.

How can I use the guide with a teacher?

Read one section, note what you understood and identify any remaining question. Review the relevant action or wording together, with the cited report available for checking.
