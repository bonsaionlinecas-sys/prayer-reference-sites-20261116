Read the heading before the rows

A useful printed timetable identifies its city, month, year and calculation settings. Without those details, a perfectly legible sheet can be used for the wrong context. Check the heading before pinning it in an office or sending it to a family group.

Find your day by its displayed Gregorian date rather than its position on the page. Months have different lengths, and an old sheet can easily remain in a shared folder. For a Hijri occasion, establish the corresponding date using the calendar or announcement you follow.

For a class or small team, ask a second reader to check the first date, last date and city before distributing the copy. Give the new version a predictable place in your files. Mark an archived version clearly so another person can tell which sheet applies without knowing how it was produced.

Notice a trend, then read the actual day

A month makes gradual changes in Fajr or Maghrib easier to see. That visible trend does not justify adding a fixed minute each morning. Read the row for the date you need, particularly when your programme crosses a month boundary or changes its working hours.

For a weekly commitment, compare the specific dates when it recurs. An appointment at an unchanged clock time may leave a different margin later in the month. Adjust the appointment or your preparation plan as needed; keep the calculated prayer times intact in the reference table.

Print a page people can actually use

Check print preview for readable type, complete columns and an orientation that fits the table. Each date should remain visibly associated with its times. If an edge is clipped, correct the layout before printing. Guessing a missing value from a neighbouring column defeats the purpose of the sheet.

Place personal notes in a separate, labelled area. A planned departure from home, an office opening time and a mosque’s announced iqamah describe different things. Clear labels let someone else use your copy without mistaking a handwritten arrangement for part of the original calculation.

Know when to replace your copy

Produce a fresh table when the city, month, year or relevant calculation choices change. Review the Isha option for dates around Ramadan. It adjusts the calculation and does not establish the lunar month. A saved setting deserves a check before you reuse it for another period.

A printed page cannot receive later changes or learn that a mosque has revised its programme. These are calculated city times, not live adhan or iqamah reports, and the sheet is not a fatwa. Keep current mosque information separately when you plan to attend a congregation.

A useful answer

Questions that help you use the guide

Is this a Gregorian or Hijri monthly calendar?

The date input and monthly table use the Gregorian calendar. For a Hijri event, first match the required day using the calendar or announcement you follow, then select the corresponding date.

Can I reuse a month’s timetable every year?

Select the required year when generating the table. The month name alone is not enough context; the copy you use should identify its year, city and calculation settings.

Why does the printout not include my mosque’s iqamah?

The table calculates reference prayer times for the selected city. A particular mosque’s programme is separate information. If you add its announced iqamah, label the source and when you checked it.

What if the table does not fit on the page?

Use print preview to adjust orientation or scale while preserving readable headings and values. Replace a copy with a clipped column or dates separated from their times.

Method and references

How is the time calculated?

Times are calculated astronomically with Adhan JS using the stated settings. City coordinates come from GeoNames under CC BY 4.0 and represent approximate centres. We do not use elevation above sea level as elevation above the horizon. Consult the locally adopted timetable and mosque announcement when they differ.
