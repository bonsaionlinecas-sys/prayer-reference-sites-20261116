Check the city before the clock

A timetable can be internally correct and still belong to the wrong place. Read the city name before using any of its numbers. If your day begins in Riyadh and ends in Jeddah, change the selected city when you review the second part of your plan.

The date deserves the same attention, especially after midnight or when preparing for tomorrow. Keep the city and date together in a saved note. A screenshot without that context can become difficult to interpret when you return to it several days later.

Try a short review before work: read the morning times, check the prayer nearest your afternoon commitments, then look at Maghrib and Isha. Identify one appointment that needs more room. The useful outcome is a practical adjustment to your schedule, rather than a list of times you have only glanced at.

Use the daylight arc to get your bearings

The illustrated day helps you follow the sequence of events and see the broad spaces between them. For an appointment or departure, use the written time. A position in the drawing is an aid to reading; it cannot establish a prayer time independently of the calculation.

Fajr and sunrise have separate labels because they represent different events. Dhuhr also has its own calculated time; the familiar twelve o’clock position on a clock does not determine it. Reading each label carefully is more useful than fitting the day to a remembered pattern.

Leave room for the things the timetable cannot know

Allow for preparation and travel when you arrange a break. A meeting that ends just before your mosque’s announced iqamah needs different planning from work you can pause readily. Your chosen margin belongs in your diary and does not change the prayer time shown here.

For a shared family or work plan, include the prayer name, city and date with the time. Revisit the arrangement if the venue changes or the day runs into tomorrow. Each person can then understand which part of the timetable the agreement refers to.

Know what the settings describe

These results use the Umm al-Qura calculation in Adhan, with a selectable Asr convention. Record that choice when comparing timetables. A calculation setting explains how a number was produced; selecting it on this page is not a fatwa about your individual religious practice.

The Ramadan option changes the Isha calculation and does not confirm the lunar month. City reference points are approximate, and the page does not monitor a mosque’s loudspeakers. Obtain its current iqamah from its own announcement before making a plan to join the congregation.

A useful answer

Questions that help you use the guide

Are these the actual adhan times at every mosque?

They are calculated times for your selected city, not live reports of mosque adhans. A mosque may follow its own approved timetable. Check its announcement separately for iqamah, the call to begin the congregation.

Why is sunrise listed between Fajr and Dhuhr?

It helps show the course of the day and distinguishes sunrise from Fajr. Listing a solar event does not add a sixth obligatory prayer or provide a religious ruling about that event.

Can I check tomorrow’s timetable?

Yes. Select tomorrow’s date and confirm the city and settings before using the results. Read that day’s calculation rather than adding a fixed number of minutes to today’s times.

What should I check if another app shows a different time?

Compare the city, date, time zone, Asr convention and Isha setting. If a meaningful difference remains, review the method and the trusted local reference you follow.

Method and references

How is the time calculated?

Times are calculated astronomically with Adhan JS using the stated settings. City coordinates come from GeoNames under CC BY 4.0 and represent approximate centres. We do not use elevation above sea level as elevation above the horizon. Consult the locally adopted timetable and mosque announcement when they differ.
